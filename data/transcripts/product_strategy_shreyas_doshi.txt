Lenny's Podcast Transcript: Shreyas Doshi on Product Strategy & Execution

Host: Lenny Rachitsky
Guest: Shreyas Doshi (Former Product Leader at Stripe, Twitter, Google, Yahoo)

Lenny: Welcome Shreyas. Today I want to talk about how product leaders build real product strategy vs just executing feature roadmaps.

Shreyas Doshi: Thanks Lenny. Most product teams operate as "feature factories". They mistake a list of features with estimated dates for a strategy. Strategy is not a roadmap. Strategy is a cohesive set of choices about how you will win in the market despite limited resources and intense competition.

Lenny: What are the core components of a great product strategy?

Shreyas Doshi: A great product strategy has three essential pillars:
1. Leverage: Identifying the critical leverage point where a small input creates asymmetrical output for users.
2. Trade-offs: Explicitly deciding what NOT to do. If your strategy doesn't make anyone uncomfortable or list explicit non-goals, it's not a strategy.
3. Product-Market Fit Continuity: PMF is not a one-time binary state. You lose PMF as the market shifts unless you continuously evolve your strategic positioning.

Lenny: How should PMs manage their time to focus on strategic impact?

Shreyas Doshi: I teach the LNO Framework:
- Leverage Tasks (L): High-impact work (like core strategy, architecture, key hires) where 10x quality produces massive business returns. Give these your best energy.
- Neutral Tasks (N): Standard work (like status reports, routine meetings) where good-enough quality (1x) is sufficient.
- Overhead Tasks (O): Administrative tasks where minimum viable compliance is optimal.

Most PMs burn out because they try to deliver 10x quality on Overhead tasks while neglecting Leverage tasks.

Lenny: How do you measure if a product team is truly strategic?

Shreyas Doshi: Look at their pre-mortems. Before launching a major strategic initiative, conduct a pre-mortem assuming it is 12 months in the future and the product has failed catastrophically. Ask the team: "Why did it fail?" This unlocks candid insights that standard design reviews suppress.
